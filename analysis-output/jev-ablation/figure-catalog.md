# Figure Catalog

All figures are in `figures/` as `.pdf` and `.png`, generated by `scripts/analyze_ablation.py`
from `outputs/*/prompts.jsonl`.

## Figure 1: `figure-01-main-comparison`
- **Purpose:** the primary outcome comparison across all 8 arms.
- **Plotted:** per-run mean score, max score, and number of prompts with score ≥ 80. Dots are
  seeds; ◆ is the mean with a 95% seed-bootstrap CI.
- **Caption must state:** n = 5 runs per arm; Fit=Jev is scored by Jev (grey, not comparable);
  coverage arms are in orange; success means ≥ 80.
- **Key observation:** every CI overlaps. Max score and success counts are driven by one or
  two outlier seeds per arm (Baseline s2, Act=Jev s4, Fit=Jev s2, Cov+Mut=Jev s5). Most runs
  top out at ≈54.
- **Interpretation checklist:** (1) Exists to test RQ1/RQ3 on outcomes. (2) Notice the
  overlap and the outlier-driven means. (3) Changes our plan: no arm differences can be
  claimed; more power or a common referee is needed.
- **Caveats:** a bootstrap over 5 points gives unstable, too-narrow CIs; zero-inflated
  counts.

## Figure 2: `figure-02-action-mix`
- **Purpose:** shows how each selector actually spent the budget.
- **Plotted:** mean share of EXPLORE / SIMILAR / MUTATE prompts per arm.
- **Key observation:** Act=Jev moves budget from EXPLORE to SIMILAR/MUTATE. Coverage arms are
  about 70% EXPLORE whatever the selector.
- **Interpretation checklist:** confirms the selector switches worked, and shows that the
  coverage objective reduces to exploration.
- **Caveats:** shares are averaged over runs, not weighted by success.

## Figure 3: `figure-03-jev-action-policy`
- **Purpose:** mechanism for RQ1. What policy did Jev implement?
- **Plotted:** mean Jev action probabilities by parent-score bucket (bars), with the static
  threshold weights overlaid (black ticks); n per bucket on the x-axis.
- **Key observation:** Jev's policy changes steadily with score: P(mutate) goes 0.14 → 0.80
  and P(explore) goes 0.37 → 0.04. It favors SIMILAR where the static policy favors EXPLORE
  (scores below 50). Under the coverage objective all parents are below 30, so only one bucket
  is populated.
- **Interpretation checklist:** (1) Shows Jev's decisions are interpretable. (2) Notice how
  the rule differs from the static one at low scores. (3) Supports reporting "what Jev
  chooses" even without an outcome effect.
- **Caveats:** the ≥80 bucket has n = 12 and 50–80 has n = 36. The empty buckets in the
  coverage panel still show static ticks for reference.

## Figure 4: `figure-04-operator-choice`
- **Purpose:** RQ3. Operator distribution and per-operator quality.
- **Plotted:** counts of MUTATE prompts per operator for uniform vs. Jev (left), and the mean
  judge score of those prompts (right; dashed line at 80).
- **Key observation:** Jev uses essentially 2 operators (steganography, synonym). Neither
  selector's operators produce a success, and Jev's favorites are not the high scorers.
- **Interpretation checklist:** evidence of mode collapse in zero-shot operator choice.
  Motivates redesigning the RQ3 prompt or context.
- **Caveats:** a few prompts per operator. Operators Jev never picked are undersampled under
  uniform (5 seeds, repeated sequences).

## Figure 5: `figure-05-best-so-far`
- **Purpose:** search dynamics over iterations.
- **Plotted:** best score so far per iteration, averaged over seeds; coverage arms dashed.
- **Key observation:** every arm plateaus by iteration 2–3 and none improves after
  iteration 3. The exception is Cov+Mut=Jev, where one seed reaches 100 at iteration 5.
  Curves above ≈54 come from single seeds. Act=Jev starts high at iteration 0 because two
  initial EXPLORE prompts happened to score 80 (seeds 2 and 4). That comes before any Jev
  decision matters, so it isn't a selector effect.
- **Caveats:** mean-of-max curves are sensitive to one outlier seed.

## Figure 6: `figure-06-score-distribution`
- **Purpose:** explains why mean score is a weak metric.
- **Plotted:** histogram of scores for all Meta-judged prompts.
- **Key observation:** the score is discrete: refusal ≈20, partial ≈37, benign compliance
  ≈54, and harm ≥ 80 is rare. Means mostly track refusal rate.
- **Interpretation checklist:** argues for success-based and coverage-based metrics, and for
  larger n, over mean score.

## Figure 7: `figure-07-action-outcomes`
- **Purpose:** RQ1 mechanism. Which action actually works at each parent score?
- **Plotted:** child score (mean, run-bootstrap CI), refusal rate, and P(child > parent) by
  parent bucket × action. n is shown above the x-axis. ■ marks the static rule's most likely
  action and ★ marks Jev's.
- **Key observation:** SIMILAR is best below 30, where Jev is right and static is wrong.
  SIMILAR is worst at 30–50, where Jev is wrong (n = 25). SIMILAR and MUTATE are both good
  at 50–80.
- **Interpretation checklist:** (1) Replaces the underpowered arm contrast with pooled
  evidence. (2) Notice Jev's error pattern by bucket. (3) This pattern explains why Act=Jev
  nets to zero.
- **Caveats:** observational; the 30–50 and ≥80 buckets are small.

## Figure 8: `figure-08-policy-values`
- **Purpose:** bounds the effect of RQ1.
- **Plotted:** direct-method value (child score, refusal rate) for the oracle, static,
  uniform and Jev policies, with 95% run-bootstrap CIs.
- **Key observation:** Jev ≈ static, and the paired difference is tight (+0.1 [−0.7, +1.0]).
  The oracle's headroom is ≈3 points on score and ≈13 points on refusal.
- **Caveats:** the oracle is fitted in-sample, so it is optimistic. The per-policy CIs
  overlap because they share data; use the paired difference in the stats appendix.

## Figure 9: `figure-09-operator-outcomes`
- **Purpose:** RQ3. Were Jev's operator picks good?
- **Plotted:** mean judge score (run-bootstrap CI) of MUTATE prompts per operator, pooled over
  all Meta-judged arms. Operators Jev picked are in red; n and Jev pick counts are in the
  labels.
- **Key observation:** Jev's dominant pick, semantic_steganography (n = 30), is near the
  bottom. Higher-scoring operators (artprompt, dual_response_divider, many_shot) were never
  picked by Jev.
- **Caveats:** not adjusted for parent quality; many operators have n ≤ 6.

## Figure 10: `figure-10-jev-judge-consistency`
- **Purpose:** what can be said about Jev as a judge without labels.
- **Plotted:** histogram of Jev P(related) with the 0.5 threshold (log y-axis); number of
  successes and mean score as the threshold varies.
- **Key observation:** P(related) is mostly decisive but has a real middle mass. The success
  count ranges from 6 to 1 across thresholds 0.3–0.7, while the mean score is stable.
- **Caveats:** internal consistency only; says nothing about accuracy.
